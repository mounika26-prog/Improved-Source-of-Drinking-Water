# 📊 Key Findings — PS-38: Improved Source of Drinking Water

**IBM SkillsBuild Internship | Data Analytics Project**  
Analysis Period: 2011 – 2016 – 2021 | Coverage: 20 Indian States, 28 Districts

---

## 🔑 Finding 1: National Progress Is Substantial But Unequal

- National average improved drinking water access rose from **~76.6% (2011)** to **~88.1% (2021)** — an improvement of **+11.5 percentage points** over a decade.
- Urban areas maintained consistently higher access: **~95–98%** across all years.
- Rural areas started at **~58–60%** in 2011 and reached **~81–83%** by 2021 — still below the 90% national target.

---

## 🔑 Finding 2: Rural–Urban Gap Remains Significant

- In 2021, the urban–rural gap in water access is **~14–17 percentage points**.
- Despite rapid rural improvement under schemes like Jal Jeevan Mission, urban populations retain a substantial infrastructure advantage.
- Rural Bihar, Jharkhand, and Odisha remain below **65%** access even in 2021.

---

## 🔑 Finding 3: Regional Disparities Are Stark

| Region    | Avg Water Access (2021) | Notes |
|-----------|------------------------|-------|
| South     | ~90%                   | Consistently highest — Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka |
| West      | ~89%                   | Strong urban performance (Mumbai, Ahmedabad) |
| North     | ~86%                   | Delhi and Punjab drive averages up |
| Northeast | ~81%                   | Guwahati improving but rural lag remains |
| Central   | ~79%                   | MP, Chhattisgarh — slower rural progress |
| East      | ~75%                   | Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha pull averages down |

## 🔑 Finding 5: Strong Socio-Economic Correlations

| Indicator          | Pearson r with Water Access |
|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| Sanitation Coverage | **r ≈ +0.97** (Very high) |
| Literacy Rate       | **r ≈ +0.86** (High)       |
| Average Income      | **r ≈ +0.82** (High)       |
| Clean Cooking Fuel  | **r ≈ +0.95** (Very high)  |
| Migration Rate      | **r ≈ +0.35** (Moderate)   |

> **Interpretation:** Water access is deeply tied to overall development. States with higher literacy and income almost universally show better water infrastructure — pointing to the need for holistic policy (education + income + water + sanitation).

---

## 🔑 Finding 6: Clean Cooking Fuel — A Co-Indicator

- Rural clean cooking fuel access improved from ~**18% (2011)** to ~**42% (2021)** — still over half of rural households depend on biomass/solid fuels.
- Urban areas are at ~**88–90%** clean fuel, widening the rural-urban divide.
- The correlation between water access and clean fuel (r ≈ 0.95) suggests these are symptoms of the same underlying development gap.

---

## 🔑 Finding 7: Migration and Infrastructure

- Higher migration rates are concentrated in **Eastern** and **Central** regions — areas with lower water access, suggesting people migrate *from* under-served areas.
- Urban areas with high migration (Delhi, Mumbai) maintain excellent water access, confirming that urban infrastructure absorbs migration effectively.
- Rural high-migration districts face compounding challenges: departing working-age population + underfunded water infrastructure.

---

## 📌 Policy Recommendations

1. **Prioritize Eastern & Central India** for accelerated water infrastructure investment.
2. **Integrate Jal Jeevan Mission with Ujjwala Yojana** — both water and clean fuel can be addressed through co-located field programs.
3. **Literacy and income upliftment** remain critical enablers: communities with higher literacy are more likely to maintain and demand improved water infrastructure.
4. **Reduce rural–urban gap** through piped water supply to peri-urban and semi-rural areas.
5. **Monitor migration-prone districts** separately — outward migration may create service planning gaps.
